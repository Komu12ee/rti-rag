Facts:
The appellant vide RTI application dated 25.03.2017 sought inspection of
the entire file in regard to the award of license for Maxi Cab services at the
Vijayawada railway station premises in pursuance of notice inviting E-tender,
dated 27.09.2016. The CPIO’s reply or the First Appellate Authority (FAA)’s
order is not on record. Aggrieved with the non-supply of the desired information
from the respondent authority, the appellant filed second appeal under the
provision of Section 19 of the RTI Act before the Central Information Commission
on 06.08.2017.
Grounds for Second Appeal
The CPIO did not provide the desired information.

Order
Appellant : Absent
Respondent : Absent
Both the parties were found absent during the hearing at the CIC.
The respondent, CPIO Airport Authority of India was absent despite valid
and timely notice of the CIC for which a warning is issued to the concerned
respondent PIO. The concerned PIO should desist from committing such lapse in
future. He should either take prior permission of the Commission before absenting
himself from the hearing or he should send proper representative to the
Commission’s hearing after obtaining due permission from the Commission for
doing so and after briefing the authorised representative duly.
The appellant was not present to plead for this case.
On perusal of the relevant case record, it was noted by the Commission that
the appellant wanted to inspect the entire records in connection with the award of
license for Maxi Cab services at Vijayawada railway station. The Commission
could not be briefed about how many agencies had responded to the E-tender
notice issued by the Airports Authority of India, Vijaywada. However, whatsoever
may be the number of firms who had applied in connection with the e-tender
notice, the fact remains that the sought for information would have included
information about turnover of these competing firms, their income tax returns, the
firms’ sales tax records and other commercial details. In case the sought for
information is disclosed i.e. the inspection of documents submitted by these firms
which took part in the above said e-tender notice is agreed to, the appellant would
have access to host of information pertaining to these competing firms which are
vis-à-vis the applicant. This sought for document inspection is therefore not only
against the provision of 8(1)(j) of the RTI Act (right to third party privacy) but it
also severely compromises the provision of section 8(1)(d) of the RTI Act which
is as follows:-

8. (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, there shall be no
obligation to give any citizen,—
(d) information including commercial confidence, trade secrets or
intellectual property, the disclosure of which would harm the competitive
position of a third party, unless the competent authority is satisfied that
larger public interest warrants the disclosure of such information.
The sought for information if disclosed may disclose certain commercial
secrets and other details of agencies which took part in the said tendering process
and such inspection/disclosure can harm the competitive position of such
agencies/firms. The Commission’s considered view is therefore that if the sought
for information is disclosed to the appellant by way of grant of permission for
undertaking of inspection of documents submitted by the above said third party
firm, the third parties’ interest will be jeopardized which is evidently not in the
public interest.
With the above observation, the appeal is disposed of.
